How it's made

Everything here is generated in the browser. There are no models and no textures, only noise, a few formulas and one HTML file.

An endless sea

The world is cut into 10 × 10 chunks, and each one grows its own karst cluster, bays and islets from a seed. Chunks near you are built at full detail, farther ones at lower detail, and the most distant fade into mist so you never see one appear. Right-drag, or drag with two fingers, to drift across the river.

Stone

Each tower is a deformed cylinder with a sugarloaf profile, an undercut at the waterline and vertical flutes carved by noise. Vegetation density is baked into every vertex, so the foliage sits on green mats with bare grey cliffs between them.

Water

The scene is rendered once into an HDR target with depth. The water reads that depth to measure its own thickness, absorbs colour through it (Beer–Lambert), bends what lies beneath, and ray-marches reflections of the towers. Ripples come from a wave equation on the GPU, in a 16-unit window that travels with you.

Raft, shark and swimmer

The raft drifts around the home bay, and a click on the water sends it there. The shark swims with an S-curve that speeds up with velocity and cuts a wake with its fin. The swimmer walks the banks, jumps, wades in, does front crawl at the surface, treads water when you let go, and does breaststroke below until their air runs out.

Wildlife

Fish schools in four species turn away together when the shark or swimmer comes close. Carp leap out of the river, mallard families paddle with their ducklings in a line, water buffalo wade and drink in the shallows, turtles slide off the banks when disturbed, herons strike at fish and fly off, dragonflies dart over the shallows, and fireflies come out at dusk. Every creature is built from simple shapes and recycled ahead of you as you explore.

Underwater

Dip below the surface and the render path switches: jade fog, shafts of light, drifting motes, and a mirror-like surface with one bright Snell's window to the sky.
